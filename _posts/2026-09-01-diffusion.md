## [](#prologue)Prologue
Images generated by machine learning models up until the end 2010s were not very impressive compared to today's standards. When diffusion models were introduced and had matured for some years, they really took off. The diffusion model architecture had more stable training, and also enabled some significant tweaks. Today, diffusion models are used to create ultra-realistic images and videos, albeit GANs are much faster at inference, and therefore still viable in that sense. This post will cover how they work, and show an example of a simple version for creating custom Pokémon sprites.

## [](#generation)Generation
A large portion of machine learning models are inherently probabilistic in that they model the relation between an observed input $x$ and its target $y$. We distinguish between ***discriminative*** models and ***generative*** models. A discriminative model describes the conditional distribution $p(y|x)$, which could be a class $y$, based on some input $x$. A generative model describes the joint distribution $p(x,y)=p(x|y)p(y)$, which describes how the data is produced. In simpler terms, the former is a classifier/regression model, and the latter generates samples from our data distribution.
